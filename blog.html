5 Heart-Healthy Habits Cardiologists Swear By

SC

Dr. Sarah Chen, MD, FACC

Head of Cardiology · Medora Health

Every day, your heart beats roughly 100,000 times — and the small choices you make each morning decide how well it keeps that rhythm for decades. After 14 years in interventional cardiology, I've noticed something: the patients with the healthiest hearts aren't doing anything extreme. They're doing a few boring things, consistently.

Here are the five habits I recommend to every patient — and practice myself.

1. Walk 30 minutes before you sit

You don't need a marathon. A brisk 30-minute walk — ideally before your first meeting of the day — improves endothelial function, lowers resting blood pressure, and sets your metabolic tone for the next 16 hours. Research published in circulation journals consistently shows that walking beats gym-intensive bursts for long-term cardiovascular outcomes.

2. Treat sleep like a prescription

Adults sleeping fewer than 6 hours a night show measurably higher markers of arterial inflammation. I tell patients: 7–8 hours is not a luxury, it's medication. Keep a consistent bedtime, dim screens an hour before, and treat your bedroom like a cave — cool, dark, quiet.

3. Add fiber before you subtract anything

Rather than cutting out foods, start by adding 30g of fiber daily — oats, legumes, berries, leafy greens. Soluble fiber binds LDL cholesterol in the gut before it ever reaches your bloodstream. It's the single most underused heart intervention we have.

4. Know your numbers by heart

  • Blood pressure: aim for under 120/80
  • LDL cholesterol: under 100 (under 70 if you have risk factors)
  • Fasting glucose: under 100 mg/dL
  • Resting heart rate: 50–70 bpm

If you don't know yours, book a screening. These four numbers predict cardiovascular risk better than any wearable.

5. Build a stress-release valve

Chronic stress keeps cortisol elevated, which inflames arteries and raises blood pressure. Whether it's breathwork, gardening, or calling an old friend — find the 10-minute daily ritual that discharges your stress, and guard it like an appointment with me.

None of these habits require willpower heroics. They require systems. Pick two, start this week, and your cardiologist will thank you at your next visit.
